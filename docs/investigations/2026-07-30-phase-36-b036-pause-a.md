**(c) What fails silently in what this touches.**

Six surfaces, all found by probing, all now either guarded or recorded:

1. **Three of four German TSO control areas publish; Amprion publishes nothing.** 50Hertz,
   TenneT DE and TransnetBW carry byte-identical values (verified: all 88 activated aFRR-up ISPs
   of 2026-01-05 identical across the three). Amprion returns "No matching data found" for every
   reserve type. **A probe that happened to pick Amprion would conclude Germany publishes no
   settled activation prices — the same false negative E0 reached by a different route.** Guarded:
   the fetcher names its publisher, the manifest records mirrors and silent areas explicitly, a
   `--verify-publishers` mode re-measures the identity claim, and a test asserts every German row
   carries exactly one control-area EIC.
2. **The series is sparse by construction and absence is not zero.** A84 publishes a price only
   for ISPs where energy was actually activated — on 2026-01-05, 88 of 96 ISPs up and 40 of 96
   down for aFRR, and 5 of 96 for mFRR. A mean over calendar ISPs treating absence as zero would
   understate the activation price; a mean over activated ISPs says nothing about how often
   activation happens. Guarded: activated-ISP counts per month per direction are in the manifest,
   the summary table reports level and frequency side by side and refuses to combine them, and a
   test asserts no row carries a null price and that the series really is sub-dense.
3. **`curveType A03` is block compression, and this was read wrong at first.** Its formal reading
   holds a price until the next position. The first version emitted one row per published point and
   asserted contiguity, on the strength of a single day of aFRR where positions happened to be
   contiguous. They are not in general: DE mFRR on 2022-06-22 publishes a five-quarter-hour Period
   carrying positions 1, 3 and 5, so one-row-per-point covered 45 of 75 minutes and silently
   dropped the rest — 4 465 such losses across the first acquisition. Points are now expanded
   across `[position, nextPosition)`, rows from a compressed block carry `extra.block_isps`, and
   the invariant asserted is the one that actually holds: expanded rows must **tile** each Period
   exactly. The worry that carry-forward would fabricate activations was unfounded — sparsity is
   expressed by Period boundaries, not by position gaps.
6. **A parallel TimeSeries pads unactivated quarter-hours with a price of zero**, and
   `standard_MarketProduct=A01` is what excludes it — see §2.6a. Guarded: any row priced exactly 0
   is dropped and counted in `zero_price_rows_dropped`, which must be 0 under the filter, plus a
   test asserting no committed row carries a zero price.
4. **The platform serves scheduled maintenance as HTTP 503 with an HTML page.** Hit live during
   this session (§2.7). A maintenance window, a rate limit and a schema change all arrive as
   "fetch failed" and want different responses. Guarded: the fetcher detects the maintenance page
   by name and backs off in minutes rather than seconds; the refresh classifies failures into
   `source_unavailable` / `rate_limited` / `schema_drift` / `network` / `unknown` and names the
   class in the PR body.
5. **The break calendar has no `manifest.json`** — `structural-breaks.json` is its own manifest.
   Under the refresh's default it would have found no manifest, audited an empty file list, and
   reported "no change" **forever**. A source that can never fail a gate is a source that is not
   being gated. Guarded: an explicit `manifest_file`, a `nothing_to_audit` anomaly for any source
   that yields no auditable files, and tests asserting every declared source has a readable
   manifest and an existing fetcher.

**(d) At which layer and time success is verified.**

- **Layer: the committed bytes, not the fetchers.** Checksums recomputed from disk, every row
  re-validated against the schema on load, and the summary table rebuilt with no network access.
  The cross-check against the German TSOs' own published number (§2.4) runs offline from a
  committed fixture.
- **Independent check (B5):** the activation series is verified against a **different publisher of
  the same settled quantity** — netztransparenz's own AEP-Modul-1 CSV — not against another view
  of the same ENTSO-E response. 78 of 92 priced ISPs agree to the cent. The 14 that differ, and
  why the disagreement is on the Modul-1 side, are in §2.4; the number is quoted in the test so
  the gap stays visible rather than rounded away.
- **Time.** For B-036: immediately and repeatably; retrieval dates are stamped per manifest
  because coverage grows. For E0.1: the proof is the **first run executed end-to-end**, because a
  scheduled workflow that has never run is an untested workflow (C7 applied to a cron rather than
  a URL). A schedule verified only by reading its cron expression is not verified.

### 2.6a The filter that makes the difference — and kills the AT before/after

Every count in §2.6 was measured **without** `standard_MarketProduct=A01`. Adding it — the
parameter the German TSOs' own page prescribes — changes the picture completely:

| Area · product | WITH `standard_MarketProduct=A01` | WITHOUT it |
|---|---|---|
| AT aFRR 2021-01 | **no matching data** | 5 896 points |
| AT aFRR 2022-01 | **no matching data** | 5 942 points |
| AT aFRR 2025-08 | 16 points | 5 968 points |
| AT aFRR 2026-01 | 5 952 points | 5 952 points |

**Why the filter is not optional.** Inspecting a DE mFRR document (2022-06-22) shows **four**
TimeSeries, not one:

| | `standard_MarketProduct` | `imbalance_Price.category` | Points | Non-zero | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ts1 | **A01** | A08 | 3 | 3 | one 75-minute Period |
| ts2 | **A01** | A08 | 1 | 1 | one 30-minute Period |
| ts3 | *absent* | A07 | 7 | 3 | the whole day |
| ts4 | *absent* | A07 | 7 | **0** | the whole day |

ts3 and ts4 pad **every unactivated quarter-hour of the day with a price of zero**. That is absence
published as zero — the Svenska kraftnät trap in a second source — and it is exactly what the TSOs'
prescribed "Type of Product: **Standard**" excludes. Dropping the filter to widen Austrian coverage
admits roughly 190 fabricated "activated at 0 EUR/MWh" quarter-hours per day per product, which
would drag every mean toward zero and report an activation frequency of 100 %.

**Consequence, measured under the filter.** AT standard-product publication starts **2025-08-31**
for aFRR — three years *after* Austria's own PICASSO accession — and **2023-06-12** for mFRR,
essentially *at* Austria's MARI accession (2023-06-27). So Austria has no pre-accession segment
either, and **no market in reach publishes a settled activation price from before its own platform
accession large enough to calibrate on**. The only pre-accession sample anywhere in reach is
Austrian mFRR between its series start (2023-06-13) and its MARI accession (2023-06-27): **49
activated quarter-hours against 5 331 after**, a 0.9 % sample — a coverage boundary, not a
before/after.

This was got wrong before it was got right: the first acquisition ran with the filter, returned 12
of 67 AT months, and the response was to drop the filter — which "fixed" coverage by importing the
zero padding. Both the wrong diagnosis and the wrong fix are recorded here because the failure mode
is general: a portal's own recommended filter is written about one market, and the reason it exists
may only be visible in a different market's documents.

## 3. E0.1 Pause A — is GitHub Actions viable?

**Verdict: VIABLE.** The prompt's instruction was to stop and report rather than build the VPS
fallback if it was not, so each condition was checked rather than assumed:

| Condition | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Actions enabled on the repo | **Yes** | `gh workflow list` → `Fetch BTD — Balancing Stack (S2)` active |
| Workflow files can be pushed | **Yes** | the authenticated token carries the `workflow` scope |
| Runtime within free minutes | **Yes, unmetered** | `gh repo view` → `"visibility":"PUBLIC"`; Actions minutes are free for public repositories, so the monthly runtime is not a budget question |
| Secret available for the ENTSO-E key | **Added** | only `UPDATE_SECRET` existed; `ENTSOE_API_KEY` was added as a repository secret. Reversible with `gh secret delete` |
| Fetchers can read the key without `.env.local` | **After a change** | they read the file only; both ENTSO-E fetchers now prefer `process.env.ENTSOE_API_KEY` and fall back to the file |
| Source reachability from Actions IPs | **Proved by running it** | not assumable — `fetch-btd.yml` documents BTD blocking Actions IPs. The first run is the test |

**A secret on a public repo, considered rather than waved through.** Repository secrets are
encrypted and are not exposed by the repo being public, but a public repo makes the *workflow*
readable and forkable, so the design closes the one path that leaks: there is **no
`pull_request` trigger**, only `schedule` and `workflow_dispatch`, so a fork PR can never run this
job with the secret in scope. The workflow contains no `set -x` and never echoes the variable, and
a test asserts all three properties so a later edit cannot quietly undo them.

## 4. Design changes forced by what was found

### 4.1 "Incremental delta fetch" destroys history — measured, in three distinct ways

E0's fetchers rewrite their entire output from whatever the current run fetched, so the prompt's
"append-only deltas from each source's last-covered date" describes the intent correctly while its
obvious implementation is destructive. The first design response was to **shard every dataset by
calendar year**, so a `--from <year>-01` refresh could only touch the current year's file. The
B-036 dataset was re-sharded by year for that reason, having first been written as one file per
series.

**That was not sufficient, and only running it showed why.** A real refresh of Sweden took the
dataset from **146 733 rows to 15 261** and the append-only gate reported **26 277 rows removed**
from `se-fcr-2025.ndjson.gz`. Three separate faults, each of which passed every checksum:

1. **The year shard is not a safe boundary.** Svenska kraftnät publishes Swedish local wall clock,
   stored as UTC instants, so the window beginning 2026-01-01 *local* starts at
   **2025-12-31T23:00Z** — inside the 2025 shard. The fetcher rewrote 2025 with that single
   boundary hour. Any source stored as UTC from local wall clock has this property; DE and AU do
   too. **Fix:** `reconcileShards()` merges each rewritten shard row-wise with its committed
   predecessor, carrying forward rows older than the window and letting the new file govern
   everything inside it — so a genuine withdrawal by the source is still caught while the
   windowing artefact is not. The window boundary is derived from the new data's own earliest row
   rather than from the `--from` argument, which makes it immune to exactly the local-versus-UTC
   reasoning that caused the bug.
2. **The manifest's `files` list forgets.** A windowed run writes `files` from what it fetched, so
   the 2020-2024 shards stayed on disk but stopped being listed — and `loadDataset` reads the
   manifest, so the dataset would have silently shrunk to one year with every checksum passing.
   **Fix:** `mergeManifestFiles()` carries forward entries for untouched shards, but only after
   re-reading each file and confirming its sha256 still matches what the previous manifest
   recorded.
3. **The manifest's *metadata* forgets, which is the worst of the three.** The windowed run also
   replaced `coverage_verification`, dropping SE's `all_zero_rows_dropped: 5137`, its
   `per_product_leading_absence` block and its 74-month coverage table — E0's load-bearing
   evidence about *how that source lies*. The prices would still have been right and the reason
   they are trustworthy would have been gone. **Fix:** `preserveAcquisitionMetadata()` keeps the
   previous manifest as the base for windowed sources and files the run's own partial metadata
   beside it under `last_refresh`; only a `full`-window source may replace a manifest wholesale.

A fourth, smaller one, found in the same run: the manifest write was **skipped on anomaly**,
leaving a two-shard manifest beside seven files — a worse state than the anomaly it was avoiding.
The manifest is now always written; only `last_successful_refresh` is withheld, because stamping
that on a bad run would make the staleness surface lie in the one situation it exists for.

**Verified after the fixes:** the same Swedish refresh reports `no_change`, all six gates PASS,
146 733 rows and 7 files preserved, `all_zero_rows_dropped: 5137` and all 74 per-month entries
intact, and **not one shard byte changed** — the only diff is the manifest gaining its
`last_refresh` block. Each of the four faults now has a regression test, and each of those tests
exists because the bug happened rather than because it was imagined.

### 4.2 The append-only assert has to ignore retrieval metadata

Comparing whole rows would flag every row of every refresh as restated, because `extra` carries
per-retrieval provenance — and a gate that always fires is a gate that gets ignored (B7). Rows are
compared on the substantive fields only (`price`, `price_eur`, `price_norm`, `volume`,
`price_basis`, `period_end`, `resolution`), keyed on series plus `period_start`.

Relatedly, the activation fetcher initially stored the document's `createdDateTime` per row. That
is the time of the **request**, not a property of the data: keeping it would change every shard's
bytes on every refetch and destroy the byte-stability the gate relies on. It was removed;
`revisionNumber` is kept, because a bump in it is the platform's own signal that a settled price
was restated — precisely what the gate is for.

### 4.3 Activation prices do not belong in the lifecycle table

The summary table's columns — peak-to-floor, saturation month, years to saturation — are
capacity-market statistics: how far a procurement price fell as qualified supply entered, and when
it stopped falling. An activation price is an event price set by the imbalance of the moment. Giving
it a "floor" and a "saturation month" would produce cells that look like the others and mean
something else, which is the category error that made the German RAM offer-curve export unusable,
in a new costume. It gets its own block reporting **level** (over activated ISPs), **frequency**
(activated ÷ possible ISPs) and **tails** (share negative, share at the technical limit), with a
test asserting no lifecycle cell is ever attached to it.

### 4.4 One path deviation from the prompt

The E0.1 prompt named the orchestrator
`tools/consultancy/data/refresh-mature-markets.mjs`. It was written to
`tools/consultancy/mature-markets/refresh-mature-markets.mjs`: in this repo
`tools/consultancy/data/` is the data tree and `tools/consultancy/mature-markets/` is the code that
reads and writes it. The filename is the prompt's; only the directory moved. Flagged rather than
done silently.

## 5. What E2/E3 now calibrate activation on — and what is still absent

**Have, measured:**

- **DE settled aFRR activation prices**, `vwap_activated`, EUR/MWh at PT15M, 2022-06-21 → current,
  with per-month activated-ISP counts by direction. Verified against the German TSOs' own
  settlement publication.
- **DE settled mFRR activation prices** over the same span — but at 9-32 activated ISPs per month,
  this is a thin series and must be quoted with that count.
- **AT settled aFRR/mFRR activation prices**, 2021-01 → current, spanning the PICASSO accession on
  both sides.

**Do not have, and E2/E3 must say so:**

1. **No usable accession before/after on activation prices, anywhere in reach.** Germany's series
   starts at its own accession; Austria's starts three years after its own for aFRR and fourteen
   days before its own for mFRR — **49 activated quarter-hours before against 5 331 after**, a
   0.9 % sample. §2.6a. E0 §5 item 5 recorded the PICASSO break magnitude for **capacity** as
   "n=2, not n=5"; for **activation** there is no usable n. E2 must either take its
   activation-break magnitude from the capacity evidence and say so, or leave the activation leg
   unbroken and say that — not build a before/after on 49 quarter-hours.
2. **No activated volumes.** A84 carries price only. Activation *revenue* is price × activated
   energy, and the energy side is not in this dataset. The route to it is known, unauthenticated
   and recorded (§2.1) — filed as a follow-up rather than acquired, because the price was the gate.
3. **FI not acquired.** Its counts in §2.6 are unfiltered, so they say nothing about
   standard-product coverage; its accession date is not in the break calendar (so segmenting it
   would violate the calendar-only rule); and its series shows an unexplained step — FI mFRR jumps
   from ~1 420 points/month in 2024-06 to ~11 116 in 2026-06, which looks like a resolution change
   and would need its own investigation. A decision, not an oversight.
4. **NL and BE stop publishing.** Both return data through 2024 and nothing for 2026-01 and
   2026-06. Recorded as measured; not investigated, and not needed.
5. **Nothing about the Baltics.** DE, LV and EE return empty on A84 with live controls in the same
   run. Baltic activation prices are not on this surface.
